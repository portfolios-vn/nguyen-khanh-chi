My international awards

Four awards in 2026, from three pieces of work I led.

Doji: valuing an AI startup

  • AXGO 2026 Gold Award

Case Study - AI & Fashion Tech: A Business & Market Analysis of Doji AI Virtual Try-On App

Entry at the AX Global Olympiad (AXGO) 2026, held 14 July 2026; Gold Award.

The question: Could a seed-stage AI virtual try-on app justify a 14 million dollar raise in a fashion market where 30-40% of online orders are returned?

My part: Led the six-part case study and owned its finance modules: the unit economics (affiliate, data and B2B SaaS revenue per user, LTV about 132 dollars over three years, LTV:CAC 37:1), the Scorecard valuation against a 10 million dollar AI seed median, and the runway, burn and Series A revenue model (about 500 brand clients for 14 million dollars ARR).

How: Five-step business analysis (market research, customer analysis, technology assessment, marketing strategy, financial modelling) using STP, 4Ps, LTV:CAC and the Scorecard valuation method.

Result: Estimated LTV of about 132 dollars per user, an LTV:CAC ratio of about 37:1, a Scorecard valuation benchmarked on a 10 million dollar AI seed median, and a runway of 24-28 months; Gold Award at AXGO 2026.

What I learned: Valuing a startup with almost no history showed me how much of any valuation is assumption. I trusted the unit economics more than the headline number, because I could check every input behind the LTV and the CAC.

Award: AX Global Olympiad (AXGO) 2026, Gold Award (International).

High-tech FDI at Hoa Lac

  • WICO 2026 Gold Award

Attracting High-Tech FDI to Hanoi: Opportunities and Policy Implications for Hoa Lac Hi-Tech Park

Research poster and presentation at the World Invention Creativity Olympic (WICO) 2026, Seoul, July 2026.

The question: How can Hoa Lac Hi-Tech Park help Hanoi attract foreign investment with stronger research and technology content?

My part: First-named author. Led the research on how Hanoi can judge foreign investment by quality rather than volume: analysed Hoa Lac's registered-capital and project figures against the Hsinchu, one-north, Shenzhen and Daedeok benchmarks, and shaped the five measures of FDI quality (technology content, R&D spending, technology transfer, value added, domestic linkages).

How: Qualitative review of official reports and secondary data from Vietnamese agencies, UNCTAD, OECD and the World Bank, with Hsinchu, one-north, Shenzhen and Daedeok Innopolis as benchmarks.

Result: By December 2025 Hoa Lac had 109 projects and nearly 116 trillion VND in registered capital, but capital totals do not show research activity, local value or technology transfer. The poster sets out six policy priorities and five measures of FDI quality. Gold Award at WICO 2026.

What I learned: Nearly 116 trillion VND sounds like success. This project taught me to ask what that money actually does: whether it brings research, skills and local suppliers with it, or only a bigger total. I still ask that question whenever I read an investment headline.

Award: World Invention Creativity Olympic (WICO) 2026, Gold Award (International).

Food companies on the HOSE

  • ICPC 2026 Gold Award
  • ICPC 2026 Grand Prize

Key Factors Influencing the Financial Efficiency of Food Companies Listed on the HOSE

Team paper and poster for the International Creative Papers Conference & Olympic (ICPC) 2026, Korea, January 2026.

The question: Which internal and external factors shape the financial performance of food companies listed on the Ho Chi Minh Stock Exchange?

My part: Led the seven-member team as first-named author. Ran the financial ratio analysis of VNM, MSN, SBT, KDC, SAB and BHN (ROE, ROA, debt-to-equity, total, fixed-asset, inventory and receivables turnover, market capitalisation) and drafted the capital-structure and financial-leverage recommendations.

How: Literature review and financial-statement analysis of six listed food and beverage companies (VNM, MSN, SBT, KDC, SAB, BHN): ROE, ROA, debt-to-equity, asset turnover and market capitalisation, set against economic growth, input costs and digital transformation.

Result: Proposed three solutions (capital management, capital structure, market development); the team received the ICPC 2026 Gold Award and Grand Prize.

What I learned: The six companies looked alike until I put their ratios side by side. A high ROE can come from strong margins or from heavy borrowing, and the numbers only made sense once I read them together, which is why our recommendations ended up on capital structure. Leading seven people also meant splitting the work so that every section fed the same analysis.

Awards: International Creative Papers Conference & Olympic (ICPC) 2026, Gold Award (International); International Creative Papers Conference & Olympic (ICPC) 2026, Grand Prize (International).

My published research

Factors Influencing Young Consumers' Intention to Choose Green Products: Evidence from Hanoi

Peer-reviewed article in Business Management and Strategy (Macrothink Institute), 2026, Vol. 17, No. 2, pp. 153-172; published 28 July 2026. Five co-authors, with Chi listed as a student at Hanoi-Amsterdam High School for the Gifted.

The question: Young consumers often say they care about the environment but do not buy green products; the study asks which factors actually drive their intention to choose green products in Hanoi.

My part: From the paper's author contributions statement: drew the research model (Figure 1, five factor groups leading to green product choice intention); developed the five hypotheses H1-H5 together with co-author Linh Ngoc Dan Nguyen; and wrote the research methods: the quantitative cross-sectional survey design and sample (253 valid responses from young consumers in Hanoi), the five-point Likert measurement of five predictors and one outcome, the SPSS 22 analysis plan (exploratory factor analysis, Pearson correlation, multiple linear regression, tolerance and VIF checks) and the regression equation.

How: Extended Theory of Planned Behavior model with five factor groups; a cross-sectional survey of 253 valid responses from young consumers in Hanoi, analysed in SPSS 22 with exploratory factor analysis, Pearson correlation and multiple linear regression.

Result: All five hypotheses supported; the model explains 63.4% of the variance (adjusted R-squared 0.634). Green product attributes were the strongest predictor, followed by social influence, personal factors, environmental awareness and government policy.

What I learned: What pulled me in was the gap between what young people say about the environment and what they actually buy. Writing the methods taught me to be suspicious of a number until it has been checked, which is why the analysis plan I wrote tests the factors and the VIF values before anyone reads a coefficient.

What I built on my own

HKD Financial Health

HKD Financial Health, a financial health platform for Vietnamese household businesses

Personal product, version 1.0.0 MVP, April 2026; live on Vercel.

The question: Most of Vietnam's roughly 6 million household businesses have no tool to judge their own financial health, and the 2026 move from flat-rate tax to self-declaration makes that more urgent.

My part: Built the whole app alone, from the input flow and financial logic to the API, database and deployment.

How: Full-stack web app: a five-step input flow (business info, revenue, expenses, assets and debt, results) with a month, quarter or year period, 10 financial ratios with benchmarks that differ by region (Hanoi/HCMC, large provinces, small provinces), 2026 tax rules built into the calculations, Excel/CSV and HTKK XML import, seven sample businesses to try without an account, and a full Vietnamese/English switch. Built with HTML/JavaScript and Chart.js, Node.js and Express, PostgreSQL and JWT auth.

Result: A working MVP, live online with its API and database connected. Results show a 0-100 health score with a rating, key figures, and six tabs: metrics, radar and bar charts, history, tax and legal, an improvement roadmap (1-4 weeks, 1-3 months, long term) and an AI advisor that is still a placeholder. A mobile UI and bank integration are planned.

What I learned: I studied Geography first, and what kept my attention was how places grow and where the money in them goes. Finance let me ask that about one business at a time. HKD Financial Health came out of that: a household business owner enters a period's numbers and sees where the business stands. The hardest part was making ten ratios make sense to someone who has never read one.
